# Content Framework

> The anatomy of every piece. When creating content, always identify these
> seven elements before writing the body. If one is missing, the piece is
> not ready.

---

## The seven elements

### 1. Purpose
What this piece is for. The single reason it exists. One sentence.

### 2. Pillar
Which of the four it stands on: Hospitality, Mentality, Professionalism, or
Discipline. Exactly one is primary.

### 3. Principle
The timeless truth underneath. The thing that was true before this piece and
will be true after it.

### 4. Lesson
What the reader learns. Stated as a claim, not a topic. ("Feedback given
late is feedback given to no one" — not "a post about feedback timing.")

### 5. Framework
The structure that makes the lesson usable — steps, a model, a contrast, a
checklist. Something the reader can hold and reuse.

### 6. Memorable quote
The one line that could be screenshotted and stand alone. Short. Quotable.
True.

### 7. Actionable takeaway
What the reader does next. Concrete enough to do today.

---

## The order of work

1. Find the **Principle** first. Without it, you have content, not philosophy.
2. Place it: name the **Part, Chapter, and Pillar** (see
   [`03-CONTENT-HIERARCHY.md`](03-CONTENT-HIERARCHY.md)).
3. Sharpen the **Lesson** into a single claim.
4. Build the **Framework** that proves and applies the claim.
5. Cut until one line becomes the **Quote**.
6. End on the **Takeaway**. Never end soft.
7. Run the whole thing through the Archivist's standards
   ([`05-THE-ARCHIVIST.md`](05-THE-ARCHIVIST.md)) before it leaves the desk.

---

## Every piece must teach

Not inspire. Teach.

Every reader should leave with something they can do.
