# Serve the Team First

> The guest feels exactly how the team is treated. Nothing more, nothing less.

---

**Filing**

- Part: I Foundations
- Chapter: Service
- Pillar: Hospitality

**Framework**

- Purpose: To correct the most common mistake in hospitality — pointing all the care outward at the guest while the team runs on empty.
- Principle: Hospitality starts inside the team. You cannot pour out what was never poured in.
- Lesson: Serve your team first, and the guest experience takes care of itself. Skip it, and no script, smile, or standard will hold.

---

## The piece

Every hospitality business says the guest comes first.

It sounds right. It is wrong.

The guest does not come first. The guest comes second.

The team comes first.

Here is why. A guest is with you for an hour. Your team is with you every
shift. The way a server speaks to a guest at 9pm is shaped by how the server
was spoken to at 4pm. Care travels in one direction — from the inside out.
You cannot reverse it at the table.

Treat the team like a cost, and they will treat the guest like a task.

Treat the team like it matters, and they will make the guest feel like they
matter too. Not because a manual told them to. Because that is what was done
to them.

Hospitality is not the performance you give the guest.

It is the overflow of how you treat your people.

## The framework

Before a shift, ask three questions about your team — not your guests.

1. **Are they prepared?** Did I set them up to win, or to scramble? A team
   sent in unprepared cannot give what it does not have.
2. **Are they seen?** Do they know I notice the small things they do right —
   or only the things they do wrong?
3. **Are they served?** What did I do today to make their job easier, clearer,
   or more human?

If the answer to all three is yes, you do not need to coach the guest
experience. It is already handled.

If the answer is no, no amount of guest-facing polish will save the night.

## The takeaway

Today, before you think about a single guest, do one concrete thing to serve
your team. Clear an obstacle. Name something they did well, specifically.
Prepare the thing they always have to scramble for.

Serve the people who serve the guest. Start there. Always.
